## Honesty

Direct virality evidence is different from child-attention evidence.

No individual study proves a viral formula. TivvleJoy therefore uses
non-numeric Engagement Readiness states and does not calculate a Viral Score,
guaranteed-view number, or other scientifically misleading composite.

User-facing language stays:

- Audience Engagement Blueprint
- Story Clarity
- Replay Design
- Pilot Lab
- Engagement Readiness
- Research-Informed Guidance

Forbidden language includes Guaranteed Viral, Viral Score, Guaranteed Views,
Algorithm Hack, Scientifically Proven to Go Viral, Addictive Design, and
Manipulation Score.

## What the research supports

High-arousal positive emotion is associated with sharing in adult social
transmission research. That is not a children’s virality formula, and it is
not treated as one here (Berger & Milkman, 2012).

Comprehensibility supports children’s attention. When the story is understandable,
looking stays more related to learning (Fisch, 2000; Lorch, Anderson, & Levin,
1979; Kirkorian, Wartella, & Anderson, 2008).

Recurring characters and formats support familiarity and interaction. Repeated
exposure to the same episode can increase involvement and comprehension rather
than boredom in preschool viewers (Crawley, Anderson, Wilder, Williams, &
Santomero, 1999).

Relevant motion and audio can guide attention toward story content. Formal
features that mark important content help children select what to watch
(Calvert, Huston, Watkins, & Wright, 1982). Irrelevant animation or
unsupported sound can pull attention away from meaning.

Safe humor and prosocial resolutions fit the intended 5–10 audience.
Prosocial portrayals are associated with kinder social outcomes in aggregate
evidence (Mares & Woodard, 2005). Fear, humiliation, injury comedy, and
anxiety-based hooks are blocked.

Excessive pace and stimulation are not quality goals. Very young children
already face high processing demands from screen media (Anderson & Pempek,
2005). Pediatric guidance treats heavy, poorly structured stimulation as a
developmental caution, not a production target (AAP, 2016).

## Age-band layering

Ages 5–7 need a clear visual objective, literal cause and effect, safe
physical humor, short sentences, and readable expressions. Story-critical
wordplay is not allowed.

Ages 8–10 may receive additional inference, callbacks, light wordplay,
layered motivation, and optional background clues. That layer must enrich the
video without hiding the plot from younger viewers.

## Pilot Lab

Three approved planning-only concepts live in the Lost Doodle Map world:

1. Goat, Don’t Press That Button! — visual comedy and positive spectacle;
   Neighborhood Shops home-base opportunity.
2. Can You Find the Missing Map Piece? — participation, hidden clues, and
   replay. No comment request.
3. The Cloud That Was Afraid to Thunder — emotion, kindness, and cooperative
   rescue, with gentle, clearly safe peril.

These are approved concepts, not approved final scripts or production assets.
TivvleJoy must produce and human-review three excellent pilots before scaling
a format. Raw views cannot select a winner. A human must authorize any later
batch. No automatic spend.
